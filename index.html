I’m Hajira Sultana, and I also go by Huda. I’m an AI and cybersecurity engineer, researcher, and builder based in Chicago. I build systems that combine software and hardware. My experience includes creating AI tools, automating workflows, securing software, and building projects with sensors, microcontrollers, Raspberry Pi, and connected devices.
I’m interested in applying AI and security to robotics, assistive technology, and devices that interact with the real world. My broader interests include safe and responsible AI, brain–computer interfaces, open source, and entrepreneurship. I hold a master’s degree in Cybersecurity from Lewis University and a bachelor’s degree in Electronics and Communication Engineering.
Beyond engineering, I enjoy building products, leading projects, competing in hackathons, speaking, and growing technology communities. I’m open to opportunities in software, AI, cybersecurity, electronics, and robotics, as well as research labs and fellowships where I can work across both software and hardware.

Research

I’m interested in building AI-powered systems that remain safe and reliable when they interact with the physical world. I want to study how robots and connected devices can make dependable decisions when sensor data is noisy, incomplete, or manipulated. This work brings together my experience in AI and cybersecurity with my background in electronics and my growing interest in robotics.

Secure Neurobot

A brain-controlled robotics project that turns a small set of brain signals into actions such as OPEN, CLOSE, and STOP. An independent safety system checks every command before the robot is allowed to move.

Real-Time Phishing Detection

A system that detects suspicious emails as they arrive and helps people understand the warning signs. It combines automatic detection with simple safety guidance to protect users from changing phishing attacks.

State-Sponsored Cyberattacks

A study of the Salt Typhoon attack on major U.S. telecommunications providers. The research identifies weaknesses in critical infrastructure and proposes ways to strengthen national telecommunications security.

Security for AI Agents
